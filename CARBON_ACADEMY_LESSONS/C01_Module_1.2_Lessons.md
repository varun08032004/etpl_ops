# C01: Climate & Carbon Fundamentals
## Module 1.2: Climate Impacts & Policy (3 lessons × 40min = 2h)

### Lesson 1.2.1: Physical Climate Impacts — Temperature, Water, Extremes
**Lesson Code:** C01.2.1
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Explain observed and projected changes in global temperature, precipitation, and extreme events (Bloom: Understand)
2. Analyze the physical mechanisms linking greenhouse forcing to hydrological cycle changes (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate regional vulnerability to climate impacts using IPCC AR6 frameworks (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.1.1, C01.1.2, C01.1.3

**Why This Matters:**
Carbon markets exist because of physical climate impacts. Understanding the physical science — temperature rise, water cycle disruption, extreme events — is the foundation for every mitigation policy, carbon price, and credit standard. You cannot price carbon without knowing what carbon does.

**Core Concept: Forcing → Warming → Impacts → Risk**

### 1.2.1.1 Observed Warming — The Unequivocal Evidence

**IPCC AR6 Key Finding:** Global surface temperature 2011-2020 was 1.09°C [0.95-1.20] higher than 1850-1900.

**Observed Changes (AR6 WG1):**
| Indicator | Change (2011-2020 vs 1850-1900) | Confidence |
|-----------|--------------------------------|------------|
| Global Surface Temperature | +1.09°C [0.95-1.20] | Very High |
| Land Temperature | +1.59°C [1.34-1.83] | Very High |
| Ocean Temperature (0-700m) | +0.88°C [0.68-1.01] | Very High |
| Ocean Heat Content (0-2000m) | +230 ZJ (1971-2018) | Very High |
| Global Mean Sea Level | +0.20m [0.15-0.25] (1901-2018) | High |
| Arctic Sea Ice (September) | -40% (1979-2019) | High |
| Glacier Mass Loss | -6,200 Gt (1993-2019) | Very High |

**Key Insight:** Land warms ~1.5× faster than oceans (thermal inertia). Northern Hemisphere > Southern Hemisphere. Arctic warming ~3× global average (Arctic amplification).

### 1.2.1.2 The Water Cycle — Intensification

**Clausius-Clapeyron Relation:** Saturation vapor pressure increases ~7%/°C warming.

**Observed Hydrological Changes:**
| Variable | Change | Mechanism |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **Atmospheric Moisture** | +5-10% per °C | Clausius-Clapeyron |
| **Extreme Precipitation** | +7% per °C (global avg) | More moisture + stronger convection |
| **Heavy Precipitation Events** | Increased frequency/intensity | Thermodynamic + dynamic |
| **Drought (some regions)** | Increased frequency/severity | Higher PET, shifting circulation |
| **Soil Moisture** | Decreasing in subtropics/mid-latitudes | Higher evapotranspiration |
| **Atmospheric Rivers** | More frequent/intense | Moisture transport + warming |

**Wet Gets Wetter, Dry Gets Drier — With Nuance:**
- **High latitudes:** Wetting (more precip, more extreme)
- **Subtropics:** Drying (subtropical expansion, higher PET)
- **Tropics:** Complex — ITCZ shifts, monsoon changes
- **Extreme events:** Both floods and droughts increase

### 1.2.1.3 Extreme Events — Attribution Science

**Event Attribution Framework (WWA/IPCC):**
1. Define event (magnitude, duration, region)
2. Simulate with/without anthropogenic forcing (large ensembles)
3. Calculate probability ratio (PR) and fraction of attributable risk (FAR)

**Attribution Statements (AR6):**
| Event Type | Attribution | Example |
|------------|-------------|---------|
| **Heatwaves** | Virtually certain human influence | 2021 Pacific NW (PR>1000) |
| **Heavy Precipitation** | Likely human influence | 2021 Germany/Belgium floods |
| **Droughts** | Medium confidence (region-dependent) | 2020-22 Horn of Africa |
| **Tropical Cyclones** | Medium confidence (intensity) | Hurricane Harvey (2017) |
| **Compound Events** | Emerging science | Heat + drought + fire |

**Probability Ratio (PR) Examples:**
- 2021 Pacific NW Heatwave: PR > 1000 (virtually impossible without warming)
- 2018 European Heatwave: PR ~100-300
- 2017 Hurricane Harvey Rainfall: PR ~3-4 (30-40% more rain)

### 1.2.1.4 Cryosphere & Sea Level — Irreversible on Human Timescales

| Component | Current Rate | Committed Change (2°C) | Irreversibility |
|-----------|--------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Greenland Ice Sheet** | ~270 Gt/yr loss | ~0.5-1m SLR | Centuries-millennia |
| **Antarctic Ice Sheet** | ~150 Gt/yr loss | ~0.3-0.6m SLR | Millennia |
| **Glaciers** | ~270 Gt/yr loss | ~0.1-0.2m SLR | Centuries |
| **Thermal Expansion** | ~1.4 mm/yr | ~0.2-0.3m SLR | Centuries |
| **Permafrost** | Thawing | 100-1000 GtC release | Irreversible |
| **Arctic Sea Ice** | -12.6%/decade (Sept) | Ice-free summers likely | Reversible (decades) |

**Sea Level Rise Projections (AR6, 2100 vs 1995-2014):**
| Scenario | Likely Range (m) | 83rd Percentile (m) |
|----------|------------------|---------------------|
| SSP1-1.9 (1.5°C) | 0.28-0.55 | 0.65 |
| SSP2-4.5 (2.7°C) | 0.44-0.76 | 0.90 |
| SSP5-8.5 (4.4°C) | 0.63-1.01 | 1.20 |
| **Low-likelihood, high-impact** | — | **Up to 2m (ice sheet collapse)** |

### 1.2.1.5 Regional Impacts — India Focus

**India-Specific Projections (AR6, IITM/IMD):**
| Impact | Current Trend | 2050 Projection (SSP2-4.5) | Confidence |
|--------|---------------|----------------------------|------------|
| **Mean Temperature** | +0.7°C (1901-2018) | +1.5-2.0°C | High |
| **Heatwaves** | +150% (1980-2020) | 2-4× frequency | High |
| **Monsoon Rainfall** | +6% (1950-2015) | +5-10% (but more variable) | Medium |
| **Extreme Rainfall** | +50% (central India) | +20-30% intensity | High |
| **Droughts** | Increasing (central/west) | +20-40% area | Medium |
| **Sea Level (coastal)** | 1.3 mm/yr | 0.3-0.5m by 2100 | High |
| **Glaciers (Himalaya)** | -0.5%/yr area | 30-50% volume loss | High |

**Economic Impacts (India, 2023 estimates):**
- Heat stress labor loss: ~5.4% GDP (~$150B/yr)
- Flood damage: ~$14B/yr average
- Agricultural yield loss (wheat/rice): 5-15% by 2050
- Water stress: 600M people high-extreme stress

### 1.2.1.6 Tipping Points — Low Probability, High Impact

| Tipping Element | Threshold | Timescale | Impact |
|-----------------|-----------|-----------|--------|
| **Greenland Ice Sheet** | ~1.5°C | 1000s yr | 7m SLR |
| **West Antarctic Ice Sheet** | ~1.5-2°C | Centuries | 3-5m SLR |
| **AMOC Collapse** | ~3-5°C | Decades | Regional cooling, Sahel drought |
| **Amazon Dieback** | ~3-4°C | Decades | Carbon release, biodiversity loss |
| **Permafrost Carbon** | ~2-3°C | Decades-centuries | 100-1000 GtC release |
| **Boreal Forest Shift** | ~3-4°C | Decades | Albedo feedback, carbon loss |

**Risk Management:** Even low-probability tipping points dominate risk calculus (fat tails).

### 1.2.1.7 Professional Judgement Points
- **Attribution ≠ Prediction:** Attribution explains past; projections need scenarios
- **Compound Events:** Heat + drought + fire + air quality = compounding risk
- **Adaptation Limits:** Some impacts exceed adaptation (coral reefs, low-lying islands)
- **Equity:** Least responsible (Global South) most vulnerable — climate justice core to policy

### 1.2.1.8 Practical Exercise: Impact Assessment
*Scenario:* A 500 MW coal plant in Chhattisgarh planning 20-year life extension.
*Tasks:*
1. Project local temperature/heatwave changes (CMIP6, bias-corrected)
2. Assess water availability risk (Maharashtra river basin, glacier melt)
3. Evaluate heat stress on labor productivity (WBGT projections)
3. Estimate stranded asset risk from climate policy + physical risk
*Time:* 45 min
*Deliverable:* Physical climate risk memo
*Rubric:* Data sources (30%), risk logic (40%), policy relevance (30%)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. Why does land warm faster than oceans? (Thermal inertia, heat capacity)
2. What is the Clausius-Clapeyron scaling for extreme precipitation? (~7%/°C)
3. What is "committed warming"? (Warming from past emissions due to ocean inertia)
4. Why are tipping points a risk management priority? (Irreversible, high impact, fat tails)

**Sources:**
1. IPCC AR6 WG1 (2021) — Chapters 2, 4, 8, 9, 11, 12, Atlas
2. IPCC AR6 WG2 (2022) — Chapters 2, 4, 10, 16 (India), Cross-Chapter Boxes
3. IPCC AR6 WG3 (2022) — Chapter 3 (Mitigation Pathways)

### Lesson 1.2.2: Climate Policy & Mitigation Pathways
**Lesson Code:** C01.2.2
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Explain the architecture of international climate policy: UNFCCC → Kyoto → Paris → Article 6 (Bloom: Understand)
2. Analyze mitigation pathways (SSPs, IAMs) and their implications for carbon markets (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate policy instruments: carbon pricing, standards, subsidies, and their interaction with carbon markets (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.1.1, C01.1.2, C01.1.3, C01.2.1

**Why This Matters:**
Carbon markets don't operate in a policy vacuum. The architecture of international agreements, national policies, and mitigation pathways determines the demand for and integrity of carbon credits. Understanding the policy landscape lets you anticipate regulatory shifts that affect credit demand, eligibility, and pricing.

**Core Concept: Policy Architecture Drives Market Architecture**

### 1.2.2.1 International Climate Policy Architecture

**Evolution of the Climate Regime:**
| Agreement | Year | Key Innovation | Status |
|-----------|------|----------------|--------|
| **UNFCCC** | 1992 | Framework convention, CBDR-RC principle | In force |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | 1997/2005 | Binding targets (Annex I), CDM/JI/ET | Expired (2020) |
| **Paris Agreement** | 2015/2016 | NDCs (bottom-up), Article 6, Global Stocktake | In force |
| **Glasgow Climate Pact** | 2021 | Phase-down coal, phase-out inefficient fossils | In force |

**Paris Agreement — Key Articles for Carbon Markets:**
| Article | Provision | Market Relevance |
|---------|-----------|------------------|
| **Article 4** | NDCs (mitigation, adaptation) | Demand driver for credits |
| **Article 6.2** | Cooperative approaches (ITMOs) | Bilateral credit transfers |
| **Article 6.4** | Mechanism (A6.4ERs) | New UN-supervised crediting |
| **Article 6.8** | Non-market approaches | Complementary finance |
| **Article 13** | Enhanced Transparency Framework | MRV standardization |
| **Article 14** | Global Stocktake (every 5 years) | Ambition ratchet |

### 1.2.2.2 Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) — The Demand Engine

**NDC Structure (Paris Art 4):**
- **Mitigation:** Quantified target (absolute, intensity, BAU reduction)
- **Adaptation:** Plans, priorities, needs
- **Finance/Tech/Capacity:** Support needs/provision
- **Transparency:** Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs)

**NDC Types & Market Implications:**
| NDC Type | Example | Credit Demand Signal |
|----------|---------|---------------------|
| **Absolute Target** | "45% reduction by 2030 vs 2005" | Clear cap → credit demand |
| **Intensity Target** | "45% GDP intensity reduction" | Growth-dependent demand |
| **BAU Reduction** | "30% below BAU by 2030" | Baseline uncertainty |
| **Policies & Measures** | "50% renewable by 2030" | Indirect, policy-driven |

**Article 6.2 — Cooperative Approaches (ITMOs):**
- **Authorization:** Host Party authorizes mitigation activity & ITMO transfer
- **Corresponding Adjustment (CA):** Selling Party adjusts NDC, Buying Party adjusts NDC
- **Reporting:** Annual under Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF)
- **Registry:** National registries → Article 6 International Transaction Log (ITL)

### 1.2.2.3 Mitigation Pathways — Scenarios & Integrated Assessment Models (IAMs)

**IPCC AR6 Scenario Categories (WG3):**
| Category | Label | 2100 Warming | Net CO2 Zero | CDR Role |
|----------|-------|--------------|--------------|----------|
| **C1** | <1.5°C (50%) | 2050-2055 | ~2050 | High (BECCS, DACCS, AFOLU) |
| **C2** | <1.5°C (50%) | 2055-2060 | ~2055 | High |
| **C3** | <2.0°C (67%) | 2070-2075 | ~2070 | Medium |
| **C4** | <2.0°C (67%) | 2075-2080 | ~2075 | Medium |
| **C5** | <2.5°C (67%) | >2080 | >2080 | Low |
| **C6** | <3.0°C (67%) | >2100 | >2100 | Very Low |
| **C7** | <4.0°C (67%) | >2100 | >2100 | Very Low |
| **C8** | >4.0°C | >2100 | - | Minimal |

**Key Findings (AR6 WG3):**
- Global GHG must peak before 2025 for 1.5°C pathways
- Net-zero CO2 by 2050s for 1.5°C; 2070s for 2°C
- CDR unavoidable for net-zero (5-16 GtCO2/yr by 2050 in C1-C3)
- Fossil fuel use must decline: coal -95%, oil -60%, gas -45% by 2050 (C1)

### 1.2.2.4 Policy Instrument Taxonomy — Carbon Pricing & Beyond

| Instrument | Mechanism | Carbon Market Interaction |
|------------|-----------|---------------------------|
| **Carbon Tax** | Price floor | Sets credit price floor; revenue for govt |
| **ETS (Cap-and-Trade)** | Quantity cap | Credits = compliance units; price discovery |
| **Carbon Border Adjustment** | Import carbon cost | Extends ETS to imports; CBAM (EU) |
| **Performance Standards** | Tech/emission rate | Credits for over-compliance (e.g., LCFS) |
| **Subsidies/Incentives** | Deploy tech | Can crowd out or complement markets |
| **Voluntary Standards** | Market-driven | Credits for voluntary claims |

**Key Distinction:** Price instruments (tax, ETS) → cost certainty; Quantity instruments (standards) → emission certainty.

### 1.2.2.5 India's Policy Landscape

**India's NDC (Updated 2022):**
- 45% emissions intensity reduction by 2030 (vs 2005)
- 50% non-fossil electricity capacity by 2030
- Net-zero by 2070
- Additional carbon sink: 2.5-3 GtCO2e by 2030

**Domestic Policy Instruments:**
| Instrument | Status | Market Link |
|------------|--------|-------------|
| **CCTS** | Operational (2024+) | Domestic compliance credits (CCC) |
| **PAT (Perform Achieve Trade)** | Cycles I-VII (industry) | ESCerts → potential CCC conversion |
| **Renewable Purchase Obligation (RPO)** | Mandatory (states) | REC market |
| **Green Credit Programme** | Launched 2023 | Non-carbon incentives |
| **Green Hydrogen Mission** | ₹19,744 Cr (2023-30) | H2-derived credits potential |

**Article 6.2 Readiness:**
- Bilateral agreements: Japan, Sweden, Switzerland, Singapore
- CCTS design considers Art 6.2 alignment
- Indian CDM projects transitioning to Art 6.4/CTS

### 1.2.2.5 Professional Judgement Points
- When a client asks "What carbon price should I use?": The answer is scenario-dependent — provide range, not point estimate
- For NDC tracking: Monitor Biennial Transparency Reports (BTRs) and Global Stocktake outcomes
- For policy risk: Model credit demand under 3 scenarios (NDC met, NDC missed, NDC strengthened)

### 1.2.2.5 Practical Exercise: Policy Pathway Analysis
*Scenario:* A client operates a 200 MW coal plant in Gujarat considering early retirement + solar replacement. They want to monetize avoided emissions.
*Tasks:*
1. Map relevant policies: CCTS, PAT, RPO, Article 6.2 bilateral agreements
2. Identify eligible methodologies for early coal retirement (none yet — discuss gap)
3. Assess policy risk: CCTS methodology timeline, PLF obligations, PPA terms
4. Recommend monitoring triggers for policy changes
*Time:* 40 min
*Deliverable:* Policy risk & opportunity memo
*Rubric:* Policy mapping (40%), gap identification (30%), monitoring design (30%)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is the key difference between Kyoto CDM and Paris Article 6.4? (Corresponding adjustments, OMGE, Share of Proceeds, centralized governance)
2. What is the Global Stocktake and how does it affect credit demand? (Every 5 years → ambition ratchet → higher NDCs → more credit demand)
3. Why does Article 6.2 require Corresponding Adjustments? (Prevents double counting between selling and buying countries)

**Sources:**
1. UNFCCC (1992), Kyoto Protocol (1997), Paris Agreement (2015)
2. Decision 2/CMA.3, 3/CMA.3 (Glasgow, 2021) — Article 6 rules
3. IPCC AR6 WG3 (2022) — Chapters 3, 7, 12
4. IEA (2023) "Net Zero Roadmap" — 2023 update
5. India NDC (2022) / LT-LEDS (2022)
6. BEE CCTS Guidelines (2023) — Market design

### Lesson 1.2.3: Carbon Pricing, Markets & Policy Interaction
**Lesson Code:** C01.2.3
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Compare carbon pricing instruments: taxes, ETS, carbon credits, and their interactions (Bloom: Analyze)
2. Analyze market dynamics: price formation, volatility, liquidity, and policy feedback loops (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate policy design choices: revenue use, border adjustments, revenue recycling (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.2.2, C02.1.1, C02.1.2

**Why This Matters:**
Carbon pricing is the primary policy tool for internalizing the externality of GHG emissions. But carbon prices don't exist in isolation — they interact with energy markets, trade policy, fiscal policy, and voluntary markets. Understanding these interactions is essential for predicting credit demand, pricing risk, and advising clients on compliance strategy.

**Core Concept: Carbon Price = Marginal Abatement Cost at the Margin of the Cap**

### 1.2.3.1 Carbon Pricing Instruments — Taxonomy & Comparison

| Instrument | Mechanism | Price Certainty | Emission Certainty | Admin Complexity |
|------------|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Carbon Tax** | Fixed price per tCO2 | High | Low (depends on elasticity) | Low |
| **ETS (Cap-and-Trade)** | Fixed quantity, market price | Low (market-determined) | High (cap) | High |
| **Hybrid (Price Floor/Ceiling)** | ETS + tax floor/ceiling | Medium | Medium | High |
| **Carbon Credit (Baseline-and-Credit)** | Project-based, ex-post | Medium | Medium | Medium |
| **Internal Carbon Price** | Internal shadow price | Set by org | Indirect | Low |

### 1.2.3.2 ETS Design — Architecture & Price Formation

**Core ETS Elements:**
| Element | Design Choice | Price Impact |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Cap** | Absolute vs intensity; trajectory | Scarcity → price |
| **Allocation** | Free (grandfathering, benchmarking) vs Auction % | Free allocation → windfall profits; Auction → price discovery |
| **Banking** | Unlimited vs limited vs none | Banking → intertemporal arbitrage → price smoothing |
| **Borrowing** | Allowed vs restricted | Borrowing → near-term price support |
| **Price Floor/Ceiling** | Auction reserve / APCR / cost containment | Floor → price support; Ceiling → cost containment |
| **Offsets** | Domestic/international; % limit; eligibility | Offsets → price ceiling (cost containment) |

**Major ETS Price Drivers (EU ETS Example):**
| Driver | Mechanism | 2023 Impact |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| **Cap Trajectory** | -2.2%/yr (Linear Reduction Factor) | Structural scarcity |
| **MSR (Market Stability Reserve)** | Absorbs surplus >833M allowances | Reduced surplus → €80+ |
| **Fuel Switching** | Gas vs coal economics | Gas price spikes → coal-to-gas → demand ↑ |
| **Industrial Output** | Recession vs recovery | 2020 dip, 2021-22 recovery |
| **Weather** | Hydro/nuclear availability, heating/cooling demand | 2022 drought → hydro ↓ → price ↑ |

### 1.2.3.3 Carbon Tax vs ETS — Complementarity & Conflicts

| Interaction | Dynamic | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **Tax + ETS Overlap** | Double pricing risk | Sweden: Carbon tax + EU ETS → industry pays both |
| **Tax as Price Floor** | Tax sets floor under ETS | BC Canada: Tax floor under WCI linkage |
| **Tax Revenue for ETS** | Auction revenue funds tax cuts | EU Innovation Fund (ETS auction revenue) |
| **Border Adjustment** | CBAM ensures imported carbon priced | EU CBAM (2026 full) prices embedded carbon |

### 1.2.3.4 Voluntary Market — Price Discovery & Policy Linkage

**VCM Price Drivers:**
| Driver | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|
| **Corporate Demand** | Net-zero commitments (SBTi, VCMI) → sustained demand |
| **Quality Scarcity** | ICVCM CCP-approved, GS premium, removal credits |
| **Policy Signals** | CORSIA eligibility, Article 6 readiness, SBTi acceptance |
| **Speculation** | Financial flows, futures, tokenization |

**VCM-Compliance Market Linkages:**
| Linkage | Mechanism | Current State |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| **CORSIA Eligibility** | VCM credits → aviation compliance | VCS, GS, ACR, ART eligible |
| **Article 6.2** | Bilateral ITMOs from VCM projects | Japan, Sweden, Switzerland agreements |
| **Article 6.4** | Future recognition of VCM methodologies | CDM transition pathway |
| **Domestic ETS Links** | VCM credits for compliance (e.g., Colombia, Mexico) | Emerging |

### 1.2.3.5 Border Carbon Adjustments — The New Trade-Climate Nexus

**EU CBAM (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism):**
- **Scope:** Cement, iron/steel, aluminum, fertilizers, electricity, hydrogen
- **Transition (2023-25):** Reporting only
- **Full (2026+):** Financial adjustment = EU ETS price - carbon price paid in origin
- **Exemptions:** LDCs, ETS-linked countries (EEA, Switzerland)

**Impact on Carbon Markets:**
- Creates implicit carbon price for exporters to EU
- Drives ETS adoption in trading partners (carbon leakage prevention)
- May reduce voluntary demand if CBAM becomes primary compliance

### 1.2.3.4 Revenue Use & Political Economy

| Revenue Use | Economic Rationale | Political Feasibility |
|-------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **General Budget** | Fungible, no earmarking needed | High |
| **Green Spending** | Climate finance, just transition | High (visible benefits) |
| **Dividend/Rebate** | Household compensation, progressivity | Medium (admin complexity) |
| **Tax Swaps** | Reduce distortionary taxes (labor, capital) | Low (vested interests) |
| **International Climate Finance** | Art 9 Paris Agreement | Low (domestic priority) |

**India Context:** CCTS auction revenue → Consolidated Fund; PAT ESCerts revenue → designated consumers.

### 1.2.3.5 Professional Judgement Points
- **Price ≠ Value:** Market price reflects marginal abatement cost, not social cost of carbon
- **Policy Risk > Market Risk:** Regulatory changes (cap tightening, offset eligibility) dominate price risk
- **Leakage vs Linkage:** Border adjustments prevent leakage; linkage creates larger markets
- **Revenue Recycling Matters:** How revenue is used affects political durability of carbon price

### 1.2.3.5 Practical Exercise: Carbon Price Risk Assessment
*Scenario:* A client holds 500,000 VCS 2022 RE credits. They want to assess 3-year price risk.
*Tasks:*
1. Map policy drivers for VCS RE credits: CORSIA Phase 2, Article 6.4, SBTi, VCMI
2. Build price scenario tree: Base, Bull (CORSIA+Art6 demand), Bear (quality crackdown, recession)
3. Identify policy trigger events with dates (CORSIA Phase 2 start, Art 6.4 first issuance, SBTi guidance updates)
4. Design monitoring dashboard for policy risk
*Time:* 45 min
*Deliverable:* Price risk scenario tree + monitoring dashboard design
*Rubric:* Policy mapping (40%), scenario logic (30%), monitoring design (30%)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is the MSR (Market Stability Reserve) and which ETS uses it? (EU ETS — removes surplus allowances)
2. How does a carbon tax interact with an ETS covering the same sector? (Double pricing unless tax credited against ETS obligation)
3. What is the EU CBAM and when is it fully operational? (Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism — full 2026)
4. Why do voluntary market credits sometimes trade above compliance prices? (Quality premium, co-benefits, corporate demand inelasticity)
