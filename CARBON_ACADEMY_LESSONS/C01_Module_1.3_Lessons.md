# C01: Climate & Carbon Fundamentals - Module 1.3
## Module 1.3: International Climate Architecture (1.5h, 3 lessons × 30min)

### Lesson 1.3.1: UNFCCC, Kyoto, and Paris Agreement Architecture
**Lesson Code:** C01.3.1
**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Trace the evolution from UNFCCC (1992) → Kyoto Protocol → Paris Agreement and explain the legal distinction between targets and contributions (Bloom: Understand)
2. Explain the role of Article 6 in enabling international carbon markets under Paris Agreement (Bloom: Understand)
3. Distinguish between NDCs, LT-LEDS, and the Global Stocktake in the Paris architecture (Bloom: Analyze)

**Prerequisites:** C01.1.1, C01.1.2

**Why This Matters:**
Carbon markets do not exist in a legal vacuum. Every carbon credit's legal validity, transferability, and usability for compliance depends on the international legal framework. Understanding the treaty architecture tells you whether a credit can be used for NDC achievement, CORSIA compliance, or voluntary claims—and under what authorization.

**Core Concept: The Climate Regime Architecture**

**1.3.1.1 UNFCCC (1992) — The Framework Convention**
Objective: "Stabilization of greenhouse gas concentrations... at a level that would prevent dangerous anthropogenic interference"
Key principles:
- Common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC)
- Precautionary principle
- Right to sustainable development
- No legal emission targets — framework only

**1.3.1.2 Kyoto Protocol (1997/2005) — First Commitment Period (2008-2012)**
Legally binding emission targets for Annex I countries (avg -5.2% vs 1990)
Market mechanisms created:
- Article 6: Joint Implementation (JI) — Annex I to Annex I
- Article 12: Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) — Annex I → non-Annex I
- Article 17: International Emissions Trading (IET) — Annex I ↔ Annex I
CERs (CDM) and ERUs (JI) = 1 tCO2e units
Second Commitment Period (2013-2020): Doha Amendment, limited participation

**1.3.1.3 Paris Agreement (2015/2016) — The New Architecture**
Objective: Hold warming "well below 2°C" and pursue 1.5°C
Key shifts from Kyoto:
- Universal participation (all Parties submit NDCs)
- Bottom-up NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions) — self-determined
- No legal emission targets — but legal obligation to prepare, communicate, maintain NDCs
- Progression principle: Each successive NDC must represent progression
- Global Stocktake every 5 years (first: 2023)
- Transparency framework: Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF)

**1.3.1.4 Article 6 — The Market Architecture**
Article 6.2: Cooperative approaches — bilateral/regional, ITMOs (Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes)
- Requires: Corresponding adjustments (CAs) to avoid double counting
- Robust accounting, no double counting, environmental integrity
- Can involve private entities with Party authorization

Article 6.4: Mechanism — centralized UNFCCC mechanism (successor to CDM)
- Supervised by Article 6.4 Supervisory Body
- Activity cycle: Design → Validation → Registration → Monitoring → Verification → Issuance
- A6.4ERs (Article 6.4 Emission Reductions) units
- Mandatory share of proceeds (SoP) for adaptation: 5% of issued units
- 2% of units cancelled for OMGE (Overall Mitigation in Global Emissions)

Article 6.8: Non-market approaches (NMA) — holistic, non-market cooperation

**1.3.1.5 CORSIA — Aviation Sector Market**
Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (ICAO)
- Baseline: 2019 emissions (adjusted for COVID)
- Phases: Pilot (2021-2023), First (2024-2026), Second (2027-2035)
- Eligible units: CORSIA-eligible programs (currently: CDM, VCS, GS, ACR, ART)
- Only for international aviation growth above 2019 baseline

**1.3.1.6 Corresponding Adjustments (CA) — The Double Counting Solution**
Problem: If Country A sells ITMO to Country B, both might count it.
Solution (Decision 2/CMA.3, Glasgow):
- Selling Party: Deducts ITMO from its NDC achievement (subtracts from inventory)
- Buying Party: Adds ITMO to its NDC achievement (adds to inventory)
- Registry systems must track authorization, first transfer, and CAs
- Article 6.4: Automatic CA upon issuance

**India Context:**
- India's NDC (updated 2022): 45% emissions intensity reduction by 2030 (vs 2005), 50% non-fossil electricity capacity, net-zero by 2070
- India hosts CDM projects (2nd largest host historically)
- Article 6.2 bilateral agreements: Japan, Sweden, Switzerland, Singapore
- CCTS: Domestic compliance market operating under Energy Conservation Act
- India's position on Article 6: Supports robust accounting, opposes transition of CDM credits without CAs

**EtherTrack Context:**
- Platform supports Article 6.2 bilateral workflows (authorization, CA tracking)
- Registry bridge designed for CA tracking per Decision 2/CMA.3
- Platform supports 6.4ERs once Supervisory Body operationalizes

**Common Mistakes:**
1. Confusing CDM (Kyoto) with Article 6.4 (Paris) — different legal bases
2. Assuming voluntary credits = Article 6 units — only authorized ITMOs/6.4ERs count
3. Forgetting corresponding adjustments — the central accounting innovation
4. Thinking voluntary market credits can satisfy NDCs — they cannot without authorization

**Professional Judgement Points:**
- When a client asks "Can I use this credit for my NDC?", the first question is: Is it an authorized ITMO or 6.4ER with CA?
- For voluntary claims: VCMI Claims Code specifies which credits qualify for which claim tier
- Article 6.2 vs 6.4 choice: 6.2 = bilateral flexibility; 6.4 = standardized, multilateral

**Practical Exercise: Article 6 Transaction Analysis**
*Scenario:* Country A (host) authorizes a renewable energy project to generate ITMOs sold to Country B. Country A's NDC target is -30% by 2030. Country B's NDC is -40%. The project reduces 1 MtCO2/yr.
1. How does Country A's inventory change?
2. How does Country B's inventory change?
3. What registry entries are needed?
*Time:* 30 min
*Deliverable:* Inventory adjustment table with corresponding adjustments

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is the legal difference between a CER (CDM) and an ITMO (Article 6.2)? (Different treaty base, CA requirement)
2. What is the OMGE? (Overall Mitigation in Global Emissions — 2% cancellation under Art 6.4)
3. Can a voluntary market credit (e.g., VCS) be used for NDC compliance? (No, unless authorized as ITMO)

### Lesson 1.3.2: Article 6 and International Carbon Markets
**Lesson Code:** C01.3.2
**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Distinguish Article 6.2 (cooperative approaches) from Article 6.4 (mechanism) in scope, governance, and credit characteristics (Bloom: Analyze)
2. Explain the corresponding adjustment mechanism and its implementation in registry systems (Bloom: Understand)
3. Evaluate the operational readiness of Article 6.2 vs 6.4 for credit generation (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.3.1

**Why This Matters:**
Article 6 is the engine of international carbon markets under Paris. Understanding the distinction between 6.2 (bilateral flexibility) and 6.4 (multilateral standardization) tells you which credits will be available, when, and under what authorization regime. This directly affects credit supply pipelines and eligibility for NDC/CORSIA compliance.

**Core Concept: Article 6 Operational Architecture**

**1.3.2.1 Article 6.2 — Cooperative Approaches (Bilateral/Regional)**
- **Scope:** Any cooperative approach involving ITMOs
- **Governance:** Decentralized — Parties authorize, report, and account
- **Units:** ITMOs (Internationally Transferred Mitigation Outcomes)
- **Authorization:** Host Party authorizes mitigation activity and ITMO transfer
- **Corresponding Adjustment:** Mandatory — selling Party adjusts NDC, buying Party adjusts NDC
- **Reporting:** Annual under Enhanced Transparency Framework (ETF)
- **Registry:** National registries + international transaction log (Article 6 registry)
- **Private sector role:** Can participate with Party authorization

*Key decision:* Decision 2/CMA.3 (Glasgow) — rules, modalities, procedures

**1.3.2.2 Article 6.4 — Centralized Mechanism (UNFCCC Supervised)**
- **Scope:** Emission reduction/removal activities under UNFCCC supervision
- **Governance:** Article 6.4 Supervisory Body (SB) — UNFCCC oversight
- **Units:** A6.4ERs (Article 6.4 Emission Reductions)
- **Activity cycle:** Design → Validation (by A6.4 DOE) → Registration → Monitoring → Verification (by A6.4 DOE) → Issuance
- **Corresponding Adjustment:** Automatic upon first transfer (host Party)
- **OMGE:** 2% of issued A6.4ERs cancelled for Overall Mitigation in Global Emissions
- **Share of Proceeds:** 5% of issued units for adaptation fund
- **CDM transition:** Existing CDM activities may transition (Decision 3/CMA.3)

*Key decision:* Decision 3/CMA.3 (Glasgow) — rules, modalities, procedures

**1.3.2.3 Article 6.2 vs 6.4 — Operational Comparison**
| Dimension | Article 6.2 | Article 6.4 |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Governance** | Decentralized (Party-to-Party) | Centralized (UNFCCC Supervisory Body) |
| **Standardization** | Party-determined | UNFCCC-mandated methodologies |
| **Credit type** | ITMO | A6.4ER |
| **Authorization** | Host Party authorizes | SB registers activity |
| **Corresponding Adjustment** | Manual (Party-to-Party) | Automatic (host Party upon issuance) |
| **OMGE** | Not required | Mandatory (2% cancellation) |
| **Share of Proceeds** | Negotiated | 5% for adaptation fund |
| **Methodologies** | Party-selected | SB-approved |
| **Validation/Verification** | Party-selected DOE | SB-accredited DOE |
| **Registry** | National + international log | Centralized mechanism registry |

**1.3.2.4 Corresponding Adjustments — Implementation Mechanics**
Per Decision 2/CMA.3, Annex:
1. **Authorization:** Host Party provides authorization letter (format standardized)
2. **First Transfer:** When ITMO/A6.4ER first transferred internationally
3. **CA Application:**
   - Selling Party: Subtract ITMO from NDC tracking (mitigation outcome)
   - Buying Party: Add ITMO to NDC tracking (mitigation outcome)
4. **Reporting:** Annual ETF report includes ITMO transfers and CAs
5. **Registry Sync:** National registries → Article 6 international transaction log

**Technical Implementation:**
- Registry API must support: authorization, first_transfer, corresponding_adjustment fields
- Transaction log must be immutable and auditable
- CA must be applied at first international transfer (not domestic)

**1.3.2.5 CDM Transition to Article 6.4**
Per Decision 3/CMA.3:
- CDM activities can request transition to 6.4
- Requirements: Host Party LoA, validation against 6.4 rules, SB approval
- CERs issued before 2021: Not eligible for 6.4 transition
- CERs 2021-2025: Eligible if activity transitions
- Post-2025: Only A6.4ERs issued

**India Context:**
- India has signed bilateral 6.2 agreements with Japan, Sweden, Switzerland, Singapore
- India's CCTS design considers Article 6.2 alignment
- Indian CDM projects: ~1,600 registered (2nd largest host historically)
- India's position: Strong on CA integrity, opposes "hot air" transfer

**EtherTrack Context:**
- Platform registry bridge implements CA tracking per Decision 2/CMA.3
- Supports both 6.2 ITMO and future 6.4ER workflows
- Authorization workflow: Host Party LoA → platform → registry bridge

**Common Mistakes:**
1. Confusing CDM (Kyoto) with Article 6.4 (Paris) — different legal bases
2. Assuming voluntary credits = Article 6 units — only authorized ITMOs/6.4ERs count
3. Forgetting corresponding adjustments — the central accounting innovation
4. Thinking voluntary market credits can satisfy NDCs — they cannot without authorization

**Professional Judgement Points:**
- When a client asks "Can I use this credit for my NDC?", the first question is: Is it an authorized ITMO or 6.4ER with CA?
- For voluntary claims: VCMI Claims Code specifies which credits qualify for which claim tier
- Article 6.2 vs 6.4 choice: 6.2 = bilateral flexibility; 6.4 = standardized, multilateral

### Lesson 1.3.3: CORSIA, Article 6.4, and Market Standards
**Lesson Code:** C01.3.3
**Duration:** 30 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Explain CORSIA's structure, eligible units, and its interaction with Article 6 (Bloom: Understand)
2. Compare major voluntary market standards (VCS, Gold Standard, ACR, ART) on integrity criteria (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate the convergence of voluntary and compliance markets under Article 6 (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.3.1, C01.3.2

**Why This Matters:**
The carbon credit an employee encounters may originate from CORSIA, Article 6.4, or a voluntary standard. Understanding the eligibility criteria, integrity screens, and convergence trends lets you assess whether a credit on the EtherTrack platform meets the user's compliance or voluntary claim requirements.

**Core Concept: Market Convergence and Standards Convergence**

**1.3.3.1 CORSIA — Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation**
- **Governance:** ICAO (International Civil Aviation Organization)
- **Objective:** Carbon-neutral growth from 2020 baseline (2019 adjusted)
- **Phases:** Pilot (2021-2023), First (2024-2026), Second (2027-2035)
- **Eligible Units (as of 2024):** CDM (post-2015), VCS, GS, ACR, ART
- **Eligibility Criteria (ICAO Assembly A40):**
  - Program governance and transparency
  - Additionality assessment
  - Permanence safeguards
  - MRV requirements
  - No double counting (registry, cancellation)
  - Sustainable development safeguards
- **Current Status:** Pilot phase complete; First phase operational
- **India Context:** Indian airlines (IndiGo, Air India, etc.) subject to CORSIA; DGCA implements

**1.3.3.2 Voluntary Market Standards — Integrity Comparison**
| Criterion | Verra VCS | Gold Standard | ACR | ART (TREES) |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|-----|-------------|
| **Governance** | Independent non-profit | Non-profit (WWF-founded) | Non-profit (Winrock) | Non-profit (Emergent) |
| **Methodologies** | 200+ (modular) | 30+ (sectoral) | 30+ | 1 (TREES) |
| **Additionality** | Project-specific + barriers | Activity + investment barriers | Project-specific | Jurisdictional (no leakage) |
| **Permanence** | Buffer pool (10-30%) | Buffer pool (20%) | Buffer pool | Reversal risk pool |
| **Safeguards** | SDVista optional | Mandatory (do no harm) | Optional | Mandatory (Cancun) |
| **Registry** | Verra Registry | Gold Standard Registry | ACR Registry | ART Registry |
| **CORSIA Eligible** | Yes (post-2015) | Yes | Yes | Yes (TREES) |

**1.3.3.3 Integrity Initiatives — Convergence Toward Quality**
| Initiative | Scope | Key Requirements |
|------------|-------|------------------|
| **ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (CCPs)** | All voluntary credits | 10 principles: additionality, permanence, quantification, no double counting, etc. |
| **VCMI Claims Code** | Corporate claims | Platinum/Gold/Silver tiers; credit quality thresholds |
| **SBTi Corporate Net-Zero** | Corporate targets | 90-95% abatement; 5-10% CDR neutralization |
| **TSVCM / ICVCM** | Market integrity | Taskforce → Council governance |

**1.3.3.4 Convergence: Voluntary ↔ Compliance**
| Trend | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| **CORSIA eligibility** | Only standards with CORSIA approval supply compliance market |
| **Article 6.4** | May recognize VCUs/GS credits via transition |
| **ICVCM CCPs** | Becoming de facto quality floor for voluntary credits |
| **VCMI Claims** | Corporate buyers demanding ICVCM-approved credits |
| **SBTi** | Corporate buyers requiring SBTi-aligned credits |

**1.3.3.5 Credit Quality Screening Framework (EtherTrack Internal)**
Every credit on platform screened against:
1. **Standard:** VCS/GS/ACR/ART/CDM/Article 6
2. **Methodology:** Applicability, version, vintage
3. **Additionality:** Type (financial, regulatory, barrier), evidence
3. **Permanence:** Buffer pool %, reversal risk, monitoring period
4. **Quantification:** Uncertainty %, conservativeness, discount applied
5. **MRV:** Monitoring plan, verification frequency, DOE accreditation
5. **Co-benefits:** SDG claims, safeguards, Cancun safeguards
6. **Registry:** Serial numbers, retirement status, cancellation proof
7. **Chain of Custody:** Project → registry → bridge → platform → user

**India Context:**
- Indian airlines: CORSIA compliance via DGCA
- CCTS design considers CORSIA/Article 6 alignment
- Indian voluntary market: Growing VCS/GS projects
- ICVCM assessment of Indian methodologies underway

**EtherTrack Context:**
- Platform enforces ICVCM CCP screening for all listed credits
- Registry bridge validates CORSIA/Article 6.4 eligibility tags
- Credit metadata includes: standard, methodology, vintage, quality tags

**Common Mistakes:**
1. Assuming all VCS credits are CORSIA-eligible (only post-2015, specific methodologies)
2. Confusing standard-level eligibility with project-level eligibility
3. Ignoring that VCMI Claims Code restricts which credits support which claims
4. Assuming ICVCM CCPs are retroactive (apply to new issuances; legacy credits grandfathered)

**Professional Judgement Points:**
- When a buyer asks "Is this credit CORSIA-eligible?", check: standard, methodology, vintage, host country LoA
- For corporate claims: VCMI tier determines which credits qualify for which claim
- For Article 6: Only authorized ITMOs/6.4ERs with CA count for NDCs

**Practical Exercise: Credit Eligibility Screening**
*Scenario:* A buyer wants credits for: (a) CORSIA compliance, (b) SBTi net-zero claim, (c) voluntary "carbon neutral" claim.
Given 5 credits from different standards/vintages/methodologies, determine eligibility for each use case.
*Time:* 30 min
*Deliverable:* Eligibility matrix with rationale
*Rubric:* Correct standard identification (30%), methodology/vintage check (30%), claim mapping (40%)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. Which voluntary standards are currently CORSIA-eligible? (CDM post-2015, VCS, GS, ACR, ART)
2. What is ICVCM's role? (Governance body for CCPs; assesses standard adherence)
3. Can a VCS credit be used for SBTi net-zero claim? (Only if VCMI-eligible and meets neutralization limit)
