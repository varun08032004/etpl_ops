# C02: Carbon Markets
## Module 2.1: Market Types & Instruments (3 lessons × 40min = 2h)

### Lesson 2.1.1: Allowances vs Credits vs Offsets — The Fundamental Distinction
**Lesson Code:** C02.1.1
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Distinguish between allowances, credits, and offsets in legal form, issuance logic, and market function (Bloom: Understand)
2. Map each instrument to its regulatory framework (cap-and-trade vs baseline-and-credit vs voluntary) (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate fungibility and price relationships across instrument types (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C01.1.1, C01.1.2, C01.1.3

**Why This Matters:**
Every carbon market participant must know exactly what they're buying. An EU Allowance (EUA) is legally and economically different from a Verified Carbon Unit (VCU) or a CORSIA Eligible Emissions Unit. Confusing them leads to compliance failures, stranded assets, and greenwashing risk. This lesson establishes the taxonomy that underpins all subsequent market analysis.

**Core Concept: Three Instruments, Three Logics**

### 2.1.1.1 Taxonomy Overview
| Dimension | **Allowance** | **Credit** | **Offset** |
|-----------|---------------|------------|------------|
| **Legal Nature** | Permission to emit | Certified reduction/removal | Colloquial term for credit |
| **Issuance Logic** | *Ex-ante* (before compliance period) | *Ex-post* (after verification) | Same as credit |
| **Origin** | Cap-and-trade regulator | Project developer + standard body | Project developer + standard body |
| **Unit Name** | EUA, NZU, CCA, KAU | CER, ERU, VCU, GS-VER, ACCU, CCC | Often used interchangeably with "credit" |
| **Cancellation/Retirement** | Surrender for compliance | Retirement for claims | Retirement for voluntary claims |
| **Price Driver** | Cap scarcity + cost containment | Project cost + quality + demand | Project cost + quality + demand |

### 2.1.1.2 Allowances — The Cap-and-Trade Instrument
**Definition:** A tradable permit authorizing the holder to emit 1 tonne CO2e during a specified compliance period.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Ex-ante issuance:** Distributed *before* compliance period (free allocation + auctions)
- **Fixed supply:** Total allowances = cap (scarcity by design)
- **Compliance surrender:** Must be surrendered to regulator by deadline
- **Banking/borrowing:** Often allowed across periods (with limits)
- **Market stability mechanisms:** Price floors/ceilings, reserve prices, MSR (EU)

**Major Allowance Systems:**
| System | Unit | Coverage | 2023 Price Range |
|--------|------|----------|------------------|
| **EU ETS** | EUA | Power, industry, aviation (intra-EU) | €80-100 |
| **UK ETS** | UKA | Power, industry, aviation (domestic) | £60-80 |
| **California-Quebec** | CCA | Power, industry, transport fuels | $30-40 |
| **RGGI** | CO2 Allowance | Power sector (11 US states) | $10-15 |
| **China National ETS** | CEA | Power sector (expanding) | ¥50-70 |
| **Korea ETS** | KAU | Industry, power, buildings | ₩15,000-25,000 |
| **New Zealand ETS** | NZU | Forestry, waste, industry (ag pending) | NZ$60-80 |

**Allowance Lifecycle:**
```
1. Regulator sets cap → 2. Allocation (free + auction) → 3. Trading (spot, futures, options)
→ 4. Monitoring & Reporting → 5. Verification → 6. Surrender by deadline
→ 7. Compliance assessment → 8. Excess → banking / Shortfall → penalty
```

### 2.1.1.3 Credits — The Baseline-and-Credit Instrument
**Definition:** A certified unit representing 1 tonne CO2e of *verified* emission reduction or removal, generated by a specific project against a baseline.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Ex-post issuance:** Created *after* reduction occurs + verification
- **Variable supply:** Depends on project development pipeline
- **Additionality required:** Must prove reduction wouldn't happen without carbon finance
- **Standard-specific:** Methodologies, validation, verification per program (VCS, GS, CDM, etc.)
- **Registry tracking:** Unique serial numbers, retirement for claims

**Major Credit Standards:**
| Standard | Unit | Scope | Key Features |
|----------|------|-------|--------------|
| **Verra VCS** | VCU | All sectors (200+ methodologies) | Largest volume, modular methods |
| **Gold Standard** | GS-VER | All + SDG co-benefits mandatory | Higher quality premium |
| **ACR** | ACCU (US) / ACR credit | US compliance + voluntary | California compliance eligible |
| **ART/TREES** | TREES credit | Jurisdictional REDD+ only | Government-scale |
| **CDM** | CER | Kyoto legacy | Transitioning to Art 6.4 |
| **Article 6.4** | A6.4ER | Paris Agreement mechanism | Not yet operational |

**Credit Lifecycle:**
```
1. Project Idea → 2. Concept Note → 3. PDD Development → 4. Validation (DOE)
→ 5. Registration → 6. Monitoring → 7. Verification (DOE) → 8. Issuance
→ 9. Registry Transfer → 10. Retirement/Cancellation
```

### 2.1.1.4 Offsets — The Colloquial Term
**Definition:** "Offset" is not a distinct legal instrument — it describes the *use* of a credit to compensate for emissions elsewhere.

**Usage Contexts:**
- **Corporate claims:** "We offset our emissions by retiring VCUs"
- **Product claims:** "Carbon neutral product — emissions offset"
- **Event claims:** "Carbon neutral conference"
- **Regulatory:** CORSIA uses "offsets" for compliance units

**Critical Distinction:** All offsets are credits, but not all credits are used as offsets. A credit held for investment or compliance surrender (e.g., CORSIA) is not an "offset" in the voluntary claim sense.

### 2.1.1.5 Fungibility & Price Relationships

| Relationship | Reality | Market Implication |
|--------------|---------|-------------------|
| **Allowance ↔ Allowance** | Fully fungible within same system | Single price per system |
| **Allowance ↔ Credit** | NOT fungible (different legal nature) | Separate markets, different prices |
| **Credit ↔ Credit (same standard)** | Fungible within standard/registry | Single price per standard/vintage |
| **Credit ↔ Credit (different standards)** | NOT fungible (different quality) | Price spreads (GS > VCS > ACR) |
| **Compliance ↔ Voluntary** | Not fungible (different eligibility) | Compliance premium |

**Price Hierarchy (Typical):**
```
Compliance Allowance (EUA) > Compliance Credit (CER for CORSIA) > GS-VER > VCU > ACR > Other
    €80-100                    $10-15                        $15-30    $10-20   $5-15
```

**Quality Spreads Within Voluntary:**
- GS-VER premium: +20-50% over VCU (SDG safeguards, stricter additionality)
- Vintage discount: Pre-2015 ~50-80% discount (pre-Paris, weaker rules)
- Methodology premium: REDD+ > RE > Cookstoves (permanence risk)
- Geography premium: Jurisdictional (ART) > Project-level

### 2.1.1.6 Regulatory Mapping

| Instrument | Governing Law | Compliance Use | Voluntary Use |
|------------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| **EUA** | EU ETS Directive | EU ETS compliance | Rare (investment) |
| **UKA** | UK ETS Order | UK ETS compliance | Rare |
| **CCA** | CA Cap-and-Trade Reg | CA/Quebec compliance | Rare |
| **CER (CDM)** | Kyoto Protocol / Art 6.4 | CORSIA (vintage-limited) | Legacy voluntary |
| **VCU** | VCS Standard | CORSIA (eligible vintages) | Voluntary claims |
| **GS-VER** | GS Standard | CORSIA (eligible) | Voluntary claims (premium) |
| **A6.4ER** | Paris Art 6.4 | NDC compliance (future) | Future voluntary |
| **CCC** | CCTS (India) | CCTS compliance | Not yet voluntary |

### 2.1.1.7 India Context — CCTS Instruments
**Carbon Credit Certificate (CCC):**
- Issued under Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022
- 1 CCC = 1 tCO2e reduced/removed/avoided
- Issued by BEE after verification by accredited agency
- Tradeable on IEX/PXIL (T+1 settlement)
- Can be used for CCTS compliance OR voluntary retirement

**CCTS vs Voluntary Distinction:**
- **Compliance CCC:** Used to meet CCTS obligation → surrendered to BEE
- **Voluntary CCC:** Retired for corporate claims → not surrendered to BEE
- Same instrument, different regulatory treatment

**EtherTrack Context:** Platform supports both CCC compliance workflows and voluntary retirement, with separate registry bridges and settlement flows.

### 2.1.1.8 Common Mistakes
1. Calling allowances "credits" — different legal nature, different price drivers
2. Assuming all "carbon credits" are fungible — standard, vintage, methodology matter
3. Using "offset" as a noun for the instrument — it's a *use case*, not an instrument type
4. Assuming compliance credits = voluntary credits — different eligibility, different price
4. Ignoring vintage restrictions (e.g., CORSIA only accepts post-2015 CERs/VCUs)

### 2.1.1.9 Professional Judgement Points
- When a client asks "How many credits do I need?": First clarify — compliance (allowances) or voluntary (credits)?
- For portfolio construction: Separate compliance instruments (allowances) from voluntary (credits)
- For risk management: Quality spreads (GS vs VCS) persist; vintage risk is real
- For India projects: CCCs have dual-use pathway — clarify compliance vs voluntary intent early

### 2.1.1.10 Practical Exercise: Instrument Classification
*Scenario:* A client has: (1) 50,000 EUAs, (2) 100,000 VCUs (2021 vintage), (3) 10,000 GS-VERs (2022), (4) 5,000 CERs (2018), (5) 20,000 CCCs.
*Tasks:*
1. Classify each by instrument type (allowance/credit)
2. Identify eligible compliance uses for each
3. Estimate relative value ranking
3. Identify regulatory risks for each
*Time:* 35 min
*Deliverable:* Classification matrix with compliance eligibility
*Rubric:* Correct classification (40%), compliance mapping (30%), risk identification (30%)

**Knowledge Check:**
1. What is the fundamental legal difference between an EUA and a VCU? (Ex-ante permission vs ex-post certification)
2. Can a VCU be surrendered in the EU ETS? (No — different legal instruments)
3. What determines the price of an allowance vs a credit? (Cap scarcity vs project cost + quality)
4. Is a CORSIA-eligible VCU an "offset"? (Yes, when used for CORSIA compliance it's an offset use case)

### Lesson 2.1.2: Voluntary vs Compliance Markets — Architecture & Mapping
**Lesson Code:** C02.1.2
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Map the global carbon market landscape: compliance vs voluntary, regional vs global (Bloom: Understand)
2. Analyze the interaction points: CORSIA, Article 6, domestic links (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate market convergence trends and implications for credit strategy (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C02.1.1

**Why This Matters:**
The carbon market is not a single market — it's a layered architecture of compliance regimes, voluntary standards, and bridging mechanisms. Understanding the architecture tells you where a credit can flow, what price it commands, and what regulatory risk it carries.

### 2.1.2.4 Critical Bridges — Where Markets Connect

#### 1. CORSIA (ICAO) — Aviation Compliance → Voluntary Supply
- **Eligible Standards:** CDM (post-2015), VCS, GS, ACR, ART
- **Vintage Restrictions:** 2016-2020 (Pilot), 2021-2023 (Phase 1), 2024-2026 (Phase 2)
- **Mechanism:** Airline retires eligible units in CORSIA registry
- **Impact:** Created floor price for eligible VCM units (~$1.5-3 premium)

#### 2. Article 6.2 (Bilateral) — Country-to-Country
- **Mechanism:** Host country authorizes project → ITMOs transferred → Corresponding Adjustments
- **Active Agreements:** Japan (JCM), Sweden, Switzerland, Singapore, Korea with various hosts
- **Impact:** Creates sovereign demand for high-quality credits with CAs
- **India:** Signed with Japan, Sweden, Switzerland, Singapore

#### 3. Article 6.4 (Mechanism) — UNFCCC Supervised
- **Status:** Supervisory Body operationalizing (2024-25)
- **Transition:** CDM activities can transition (Decision 3/CMA.3)
- **Units:** A6.4ERs with automatic CAs, 2% OMGE, 5% SoP
- **Impact:** Will become new global compliance benchmark

#### 4. Domestic Links — ETS Interoperability
| Link | Status | Mechanism |
|------|--------|-----------|
| EU ETS ↔ Swiss ETS | Linked 2020 | Mutual recognition |
| CA ↔ Quebec | Linked 2014 | Joint auctions, shared registry |
| CA ↔ Washington | Linked 2023 | Mutual recognition |
| Korea ↔ China | Exploratory | MOU signed |

#### 5. CCTS ↔ Voluntary (India)
- CCCs can be used for CCTS compliance OR voluntary retirement
- Same instrument, different regulatory treatment
- Bridge: BEE registry ↔ IEX/PXIL ↔ voluntary retirement

### 2.1.2.7 Strategic Implications for Credit Strategy

| Strategy | Compliance Focus | Voluntary Focus |
|----------|------------------|-----------------|
| **Supply Development** | Target compliance-eligible methodologies (CCTS, Art 6.4) | Target premium standards (GS, ART), co-benefits |
| **Price Hedging** | Allowance futures (EUA, CCA) | Quality spreads, vintage diversification |
| **Regulatory Risk** | Policy changes (cap tightening, MSR) | Standard changes (ICVCM, VCMI), reputational |
| **Liquidity** | High (ETS futures, spot) | Lower (OTC, exchange emerging) |
| **Timing** | Compliance cycles (annual) | Continuous, claim-driven |

### 2.1.2.8 Common Mistakes
1. Treating VCM as "unregulated" — standards impose rigorous rules
2. Assuming compliance credits always trade at premium — quality discounts exist
3. Ignoring that VCM supply feeds compliance (CORSIA, Art 6) → linked prices
4. Assuming domestic ETS = isolated — linking creates price convergence
5. Ignoring that voluntary demand drives project development that feeds compliance

### 2.1.2.9 Professional Judgement Points
- When a client asks "Where should I buy?": Map their use case (compliance vs voluntary) to eligible instruments
- For Indian projects: CCTS compliance pathway vs voluntary export — different buyer pools, prices
- For Article 6.2: Host country authorization is the bottleneck — engage early
- For CORSIA: Vintage eligibility is time-limited — monitor phase transitions

### Lesson 2.1.3: Market Evolution — CDM → VCS/GS → CORSIA → Article 6
**Lesson Code:** C02.1.3
**Duration:** 40 minutes
**Lesson Type:** document
**Tier:** foundation

**Learning Objectives:**
1. Trace the historical evolution from CDM to modern voluntary standards to Article 6 (Bloom: Understand)
2. Analyze how each generation addressed predecessor failures (Bloom: Analyze)
3. Evaluate the trajectory toward a unified global carbon market architecture (Bloom: Evaluate)

**Prerequisites:** C02.1.1, C02.1.2

**Why This Matters:**
Today's carbon market is the product of 25+ years of institutional evolution. Each generation — CDM → VCS/GS → CORSIA → Article 6 — solved specific failures of its predecessor while creating new complexities. Understanding this trajectory lets you anticipate where the market is heading and position credits strategically.

**Core Concept: Iterative Institutional Evolution**

### 2.1.3.1 Generation 1: Kyoto CDM (1997-2020) — The Pioneer
**Legal Basis:** Kyoto Protocol Article 12
**Operational:** 2001-2020 (1st commitment period 2008-12, 2nd 2013-20)

**Achievements:**
- First global project-based crediting mechanism
- ~8,200 registered projects, ~2.1 billion CERs issued
- Created global infrastructure: DOEs, methodologies, registry, EB governance
- Mobilized ~$300B+ in climate finance to developing countries
- Established concepts: additionality, baseline, MRV, DOEs

**Systemic Failures:**
| Failure | Root Cause | Consequence |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Demand Collapse** | EU ETS Phase 3 banned CERs (2013); weak 2nd KP demand | CER price €20 → €0.20; pipeline froze |
| **Additionality Failures** | HFC-23, N2O destruction (perverse incentives); large hydro/RE in favorable policy | Billions of low-quality CERs; credibility crisis |
| **Methodological Rigor** | Tool proliferation without quality control; gaming of standardized baselines | Low-quality credits flooded market |
| **Governance Gaps** | EB politicized; DOE oversight weak; stakeholder consultation perfunctory | Legitimacy erosion |
| **Post-2012 Uncertainty** | No clear post-Kyoto demand signal until Paris | Investment paralysis |

**CDM Legacy — What Survives:**
- Project cycle architecture (PDD → Val → Reg → Mon → Ver → Iss)
- DOE/VVB model
- Methodology Panel concept
- Standardized baseline tools
- Stakeholder consultation requirements
- Registry infrastructure

**What Was Discarded/Reformed in Art 6.4:**
| Preserved | Discarded/Reformed |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Project cycle | Kyoto annex differentiation |
| DOE/VVB model | Unlimited crediting periods |
| Methodology Panel | Unlimited renewals without re-validation |
| Standardized baselines | HFC-23/N2O projects |
| Stakeholder consultation | No corresponding adjustments |
| Sustainable development | No OMGE (2% cancellation) |
| Registry infrastructure | No Share of Proceeds (5%) |

### 2.1.3.2 Generation 2: Voluntary Standards (2005-present) — Quality Competition
**Catalyst:** CDM demand collapse → corporate voluntary demand filled vacuum

**Verra VCS (2005):**
- Modular methodology framework (200+ methods)
- Innovation: AFOLU methodologies, nested jurisdictional approaches
- Market share: ~60% of VCM issuance

**Gold Standard (2003):**
- WWF-founded, mandatory SDG co-benefits
- Stricter additionality (barrier analysis + investment test)
- Market share: ~25%, quality premium

**ACR (1996→2010):**
- US-focused, California compliance linkage
- Innovation: Improved forest management, rice methane

**ART/TREES (2019):**
- Jurisdictional REDD+ only (no project-level)
- TREES standard v2.0 (2023)
- Emergent-backed, government partnerships

**Evolutionary Pressure:** ICVCM CCPs (2023) → standards competing *above* quality floor

### 2.1.3.3 Generation 3: CORSIA (2016-present) — Sectoral Compliance Demand
**Catalyst:** ICAO Assembly A39 (2016) — carbon-neutral growth from 2020

**Eligibility Evolution:**
- **Pilot (2021-23):** CDM (post-2015), VCS, GS
- **Phase 1 (2024-26):** Added ACR, ART
- **Phase 2 (2027-35):** Full eligibility criteria per A40

**Impact on VCM:**
- Created first compliance demand for voluntary credits
- Established eligibility criteria as quality filter
- CORSIA-eligible premium: ~$1.5-3/tCO2e over non-eligible

### 2.1.3.4 Generation 4: Article 6 (2021-present) — The Paris Architecture

#### Article 6.2 — Cooperative Approaches
- **Bilateral/regional:** Japan JCM, Switzerland, Sweden, Singapore agreements
- **ITMOs:** First transfers occurring (2023-24)
- **Corresponding Adjustments:** First test of CA implementation
- **Private sector role:** Authorized by Parties, tracked in registries

#### Article 6.4 — Centralized Mechanism
- **Supervisory Body:** Operational since 2023
- **Methodologies:** First approvals expected 2024-25
- **CDM Transition:** ~300 activities in pipeline
- **First A6.4ERs:** Expected 2025-26

#### Article 6.8 — Non-Market Approaches
- Holistic cooperation (finance, tech, capacity building)
- No credit generation

### 2.1.3.5 Evolutionary Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | CDM (Gen 1) | VCS/GS (Gen 2) | CORSIA (Gen 3) | Art 6.4 (Gen 4) |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **Governance** | UNFCCC EB | Independent non-profits | ICAO + Standards | UNFCCC Supervisory Body |
| **Scope** | Non-Annex I only | Global, all sectors | Aviation only | Global, all sectors |
| **Units** | CER | VCU, GS-VER, etc. | Eligible units | A6.4ER |
| **Additionality** | Barrier/Investment | Project-specific + standardized | Standard-dependent | SB-approved methods |

### 2.1.3.9 Common Mistakes
1. Assuming Article 6.4 = "CDM 2.0" — governance, CA, OMGE, SoP are fundamentally different
2. Assuming voluntary market = unregulated — ICVCM/VCMI create de facto regulation
3. Ignoring that CORSIA eligibility is time-limited (vintage windows)
4. Assuming voluntary credits can always "upgrade" to compliance — only with authorization + CA
5. Treating each market in isolation — they're converging via shared supply and demand
